What Makes a Great Cover Letter?

A great cover letter complements your resume by providing context, demonstrating genuine interest, and showcasing your communication skills. It should tell a story about why you're the ideal candidate for this specific role at this specific company.

Cover Letter vs. Resume

  • Resume: Facts, achievements, and qualifications
  • Cover Letter: Narrative, motivation, and cultural fit

When to Include a Cover Letter

  • When specifically requested in the job posting
  • When changing careers and need to explain your transition
  • When you have a genuine connection to the company
  • When addressing gaps or concerns in your background
  • When applying to smaller companies where personal connection matters

Cover Letter Structure

Header

Your contact information, date, and recipient's details

Salutation

Professional greeting (ideally to a specific person)

Opening Paragraph

State the position, express enthusiasm, and hook the reader

Body Paragraphs (2-3)

Demonstrate fit through specific examples and achievements

Closing Paragraph

Reiterate interest, call to action, and thank the reader

Sign-off

Professional closing and signature

Cover Letter Best Practices

Do's

  • Research the company and personalize each letter
  • Address a specific person when possible
  • Include specific examples and achievements
  • Keep it concise (300-400 words)
  • Match the tone to the company culture
  • Proofread carefully for errors
  • Include a clear call to action
  • Send as PDF unless otherwise specified

Don'ts

  • Repeat your resume verbatim
  • Use generic templates without customization
  • Focus on what the company can do for you
  • Include irrelevant personal information
  • Use clichés or overused phrases
  • Exceed one page
  • Use informal language or slang
  • Forget to proofread
