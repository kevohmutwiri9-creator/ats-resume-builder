Why Resume Formatting Matters for ATS

Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) use parsing algorithms to extract information from resumes. Complex formatting can confuse these systems, causing your resume to be rejected or misinterpreted. Proper formatting ensures your qualifications are accurately captured and presented to recruiters.

How ATS Parsers Work

  • Text extraction: Converts documents to plain text for processing
  • Pattern recognition: Identifies standard sections and data patterns
  • Keyword matching: Scans for specific terms and skills
  • Categorization: Sorts information into standard fields
  • Scoring: Ranks resumes based on match to job requirements

Layout and Structure

Use a clean, single-column layout

ATS systems parse text best when the resume reads top-to-bottom in a linear fashion. Multi-column layouts often cause content to be read out of sequence or missed entirely.

Layout Best Practices

  • Single-column layout only
  • Left-aligned text (avoid center or right alignment)
  • Consistent spacing between sections
  • Minimum 0.5 inch margins on all sides
  • Clear visual hierarchy with headings
  • Avoid text boxes and floating elements
  • Don't use headers or footers for important content

Fonts and Typography

Use standard, ATS-friendly fonts

Standard fonts are universally recognized by ATS systems and ensure consistent rendering across different platforms.

Recommended Fonts

  • Sans-serif: Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Verdana, Tahoma
  • Serif: Times New Roman, Georgia, Garamond

Font Size Guidelines

  • Body text: 10-12 points
  • Section headers: 14-16 points
  • Name: 18-24 points
  • Contact info: 10-11 points

Typography Best Practices

  • Use 1-2 font families maximum (one for headers, one for body)
  • Avoid decorative or custom fonts
  • Don't use all-caps for body text
  • Use bold sparingly for emphasis only
  • Avoid excessive italics or underlining
  • Ensure consistent font usage throughout

Section Headings and Organization

Use standard headings

Summary, Experience, Education, Skills are safe and recognizable by ATS systems. Creative headings may cause sections to be misclassified or missed.

Standard Section Names

  • ✅ "Experience" or "Work Experience"
  • ✅ "Education"
  • ✅ "Skills"
  • ✅ "Summary" or "Professional Summary"
  • ❌ "What I've Done"
  • ❌ "My Journey"
  • ❌ "Learning & Growth"

Optimal Section Order

  1. Contact Information
  2. Professional Summary
  3. Skills (optional but recommended)
  4. Work Experience
  5. Education
  6. Certifications (if relevant)

Elements to Avoid

Avoid tables and icons

Tables, icons, and graphics can break parsing and hide content from ATS. Even simple tables used for layout can cause significant parsing errors.

Problematic Elements

  • Tables (even for layout)
  • Text boxes and shapes
  • Icons and symbols
  • Images and photos
  • Charts and graphs
  • SmartArt and decorative elements
  • Headers and footers
  • Page borders and backgrounds
  • Watermarks
  • Hyperlinks in headers/footers

Bullet Points and Lists

Use bullet points

Bullets improve scanability for both ATS and human readers. Include measurable outcomes when possible.

Bullet Point Best Practices

  • Use standard bullet characters (•, -, or *)
  • Avoid custom or decorative bullets
  • Keep bullets to 1-2 lines maximum
  • Use 5-7 bullets per job entry
  • Start with strong action verbs
  • Include quantifiable results
  • Maintain parallel structure
  • Use consistent indentation

Date Formatting

Consistent date formats

Inconsistent date formatting confuses ATS parsers and can lead to misinterpretation of your work history.

Recommended Date Formats

  • Month Year (e.g., "January 2020")
  • MM/YYYY (e.g., "01/2020")
  • Month YYYY (e.g., "January 2020")

Date Formatting Best Practices

  • Use the same format throughout your resume
  • Avoid abbreviations like "Jan" or "Jan '20"
  • For current positions, use "Present" or "Current"
  • Include both start and end dates
  • Place dates consistently (typically right-aligned or after company/location)

Contact Information Formatting

Contact Info Best Practices

  • Place at the very top of your resume
  • Include: Name, phone, email, LinkedIn, location (city, state)
  • Use professional email address
  • Include full phone number with area code
  • Use clickable links for LinkedIn and portfolio
  • Avoid personal details like age, marital status, or photo
  • Don't place contact info in headers or footers

File Format and Export

Export and verify

After exporting to PDF, copy-paste the PDF into a text editor. If it looks clean, it's usually ATS-safe.

File Format Recommendations

  • Best: PDF (most universally accepted)
  • Good: DOCX (Word document)
  • Avoid: DOC (older Word format), TXT, RTF, image formats

File Naming

  • Use: FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf
  • Avoid: Resume.pdf, My Resume.pdf, or generic names
  • Include your name for easy identification
  • Use underscores instead of spaces
  • Avoid special characters

Export Verification Checklist

  • PDF text is readable when copied to Notepad
  • No strange characters or formatting artifacts
  • Section headers are clearly identifiable
  • Contact information is easily found
  • Bullet points maintain their structure
  • Dates are in consistent format
  • No missing or scrambled text

Color and Visual Elements

Color Guidelines

  • Use black text on white background (safest for ATS)
  • If using color, limit to 1-2 accent colors maximum
  • Ensure high contrast between text and background
  • Avoid color for critical information
  • Test by printing in black and white
  • Avoid background colors or patterns

Length and Content Density

Resume Length Guidelines

  • Entry-level (0-3 years): 1 page
  • Mid-level (3-10 years): 1-2 pages
  • Senior-level (10+ years): 2 pages maximum
  • Executive: 2 pages maximum

Content Density Best Practices

  • Use white space effectively for readability
  • Avoid overcrowding with too much text
  • Focus on recent and relevant experience
  • Remove outdated or irrelevant information
  • Keep descriptions concise and impactful
  • Use bullet points instead of paragraphs

Mobile Optimization

Mobile-Friendly Formatting

  • Ensure readability on small screens
  • Use larger fonts (minimum 11pt)
  • Avoid complex layouts that don't scale
  • Test on mobile devices before submitting
  • Consider mobile-first design principles

Final Formatting Checklist

Before submitting your resume, verify:

  • ✅ Single-column layout
  • ✅ Standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman)
  • ✅ Consistent section headings
  • ✅ No tables, text boxes, or graphics
  • ✅ Standard bullet points
  • ✅ Consistent date format
  • ✅ Contact info in body, not headers/footers
  • ✅ Black text on white background
  • ✅ Appropriate length for experience level
  • ✅ Saved as PDF or DOCX
  • ✅ Professional file name
  • ✅ Tested by copying to text editor
  • ✅ No spelling or grammatical errors
