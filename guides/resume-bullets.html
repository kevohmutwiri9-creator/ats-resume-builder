What Makes a Great Resume Bullet?

Effective resume bullet points are specific, quantifiable, and results-oriented. They demonstrate your impact rather than just listing your responsibilities. The best bullets follow a clear structure that makes your achievements immediately apparent to recruiters and ATS systems.

The Bullet Point Formula

Action Verb + What You Did + How You Did It + Measurable Result

Why This Formula Works

  • Action verbs: Show initiative and leadership
  • Specific context: Demonstrates scope and complexity
  • Methodology: Shows your approach and problem-solving
  • Measurable results: Proves impact and value
  • ATS optimization: Includes keywords and quantifiable data

Common Bullet Point Mistakes to Avoid

  • Vague responsibilities: "Responsible for managing team" without specific achievements
  • No metrics: Failing to quantify impact with numbers or percentages
  • Passive voice: "Was responsible for" instead of active "Led" or "Managed"
  • Too long: Bullets over 2 lines are hard to scan
  • Internal jargon: Using terms only your company understands
  • Listing duties instead of achievements: "Answered phones" vs "Managed 50+ daily calls with 95% satisfaction rate"
  • Inconsistent tense: Mixing past and present tense within the same section
  • Weak verbs: "Helped," "Worked on," "Participated in" without showing your specific contribution

How to Quantify When You Don't Have Numbers

Not every achievement has obvious metrics. Here's how to quantify when numbers aren't readily available:

  • Time savings: "Reduced weekly meeting time by 2 hours through agenda optimization"
  • Team size: "Led team of 12 across 3 departments"
  • Frequency: "Delivered weekly reports to 25 stakeholders"
  • Scope: "Managed $500K annual budget across 5 projects"
  • Ratings: "Achieved 95% positive feedback from training participants"
  • Comparisons: "Fastest project completion in department history"
  • Adoption: "Achieved 80% user adoption within 3 months of launch"
