How to Write an ATS-Friendly Resume

1) Keep formatting simple

Use a single-column layout, clean headings, and standard fonts. Avoid tables, icons, and graphics that can break parsing. ATS systems work best with straightforward text formatting that they can easily parse and categorize.

Best Practices:

  • Use standard fonts like Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, or Georgia
  • Keep font size between 10-12 points for body text
  • Use bold for section headers, but avoid excessive styling
  • Single-column layout works better than multi-column designs
  • Avoid text boxes, headers, footers, and tables
  • Use standard bullet points (• or -) instead of custom symbols
  • Maintain consistent spacing throughout the document
  • Keep margins at least 0.5 inches on all sides

Why Simple Formatting Matters

ATS parsers use optical character recognition (OCR) and text extraction algorithms that struggle with complex layouts. When your resume has two columns, tables, or decorative elements, the parser may read content out of order or miss sections entirely. Simple formatting ensures your information is extracted accurately and presented to recruiters in the intended sequence.

2) Use standard sections

Recommended: Summary, Experience, Education, Skills. ATS systems recognize these reliably and automatically categorize your information. Non-standard section names can confuse the parsing algorithm.

Optimal Section Order:

  1. Contact Information - Name, phone, email, LinkedIn
  2. Professional Summary - 2-3 sentence overview
  3. Work Experience - Reverse chronological order
  4. Education - Degrees and certifications
  5. Skills - Technical and soft skills
  6. Optional: Projects, Certifications, Languages

Standard Section Names to Use

  • ✅ "Experience" or "Work Experience"
  • ✅ "Education"
  • ✅ "Skills"
  • ✅ "Summary" or "Professional Summary"
  • ❌ "What I've Done"
  • ❌ "My Journey"
  • ❌ "Learning & Growth"

Why Standard Sections Matter

ATS systems are trained on millions of resumes and learn to recognize standard section headings. When you use creative or non-standard headings, the parser may misclassify your content or miss it entirely. Standard headings ensure your information is correctly categorized and easily searchable by recruiters.

3) Write impact-first bullets

Use action + outcome. Add numbers: time saved, revenue gained, latency reduced, tickets resolved. Quantifiable achievements demonstrate real impact and help you stand out from other candidates.

Formula for Success:

Action Verb + What You Did + Measurable Result

Examples:

  • Before: "Responsible for managing social media accounts"
  • After: "Increased social media engagement by 45% through strategic content planning and community management"
  • Before: "Helped with software development"
  • After: "Developed 5 new features that reduced customer support tickets by 30% and improved user satisfaction scores"
  • Before: "Led sales team"
  • After: "Led sales team of 12, achieving 127% of annual quota and growing territory revenue by $2.3M"
  • Before: "Improved customer service"
  • After: "Reduced customer response time from 24 hours to 2 hours, increasing customer satisfaction scores from 72 to 89"

Types of Metrics to Include

  • Revenue: Sales generated, cost savings, profit increases
  • Efficiency: Time saved, processes improved, productivity gains
  • Quality: Error reduction, satisfaction scores, defect rates
  • Growth: User acquisition, market share expansion, team growth
  • Impact: Projects completed, people trained, systems implemented

4) Match keywords (truthfully)

Use relevant keywords from the job description where they honestly apply. The goal is clarity and alignment, not keyword stuffing. ATS systems scan for specific terms and skills mentioned in job postings.

Keyword Strategy:

  • Extract key terms from the job description
  • Include industry-standard terminology
  • Mention specific software, tools, and methodologies
  • Use both acronyms and full terms (e.g., "SEO" and "Search Engine Optimization")
  • Avoid keyword stuffing - only include relevant skills
  • Place keywords naturally in context, not as isolated lists
  • Include variations and related terms where appropriate

How to Find the Right Keywords

  1. Analyze the job description: Highlight repeated terms and required skills
  2. Research industry standards: Look at similar job postings for common terminology
  3. Review company websites: See what language they use to describe roles
  4. Check LinkedIn profiles: Look at professionals in similar roles
  5. Use keyword tools: Utilize job posting analysis tools for insights

Keyword Placement Strategy

  • Professional Summary: Include 3-5 key terms
  • Skills Section: Dedicated keyword list
  • Experience Section: Weave keywords into achievement bullets
  • Education: Relevant coursework or specializations

5) Export and verify

After exporting to PDF, copy-paste the PDF text into a plain text editor. If it remains readable, you're likely ATS-safe. This simple test can save your resume from being rejected due to formatting issues.

ATS Compatibility Checklist:

  • PDF text is readable when copied to Notepad
  • No strange characters or formatting artifacts
  • Section headers are clearly identifiable
  • Contact information is easily found
  • Bullet points maintain their structure
  • Dates are in consistent format
  • No missing or scrambled text
  • Tables (if any) are readable

Common ATS Mistakes to Avoid

  • Fancy formatting: Multiple columns, text boxes, graphics
  • Non-standard fonts: Custom or decorative fonts
  • Headers/footers: ATS often can't read this content
  • Tables: Can scramble your data during parsing
  • Images: Photos and logos are ignored
  • File naming: Use "FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf"
  • Inconsistent dates: Mix of formats confuses parsers
  • Missing contact info: Essential for follow-up
  • Over-styling: Too much bold, italics, or underlining
  • Embedded objects: Charts, graphs, or SmartArt

Advanced ATS Optimization Techniques

Skills Section Optimization

Create a dedicated skills section near the top of your resume, after your summary. Group skills logically:

  • Technical Skills: Software, programming languages, tools
  • Soft Skills: Communication, leadership, problem-solving
  • Industry Skills: Domain-specific knowledge and certifications
  • Languages: Language proficiency levels

Experience Section Best Practices

  • Use reverse chronological order (most recent first)
  • Include company name, location, job title, and dates
  • Use consistent date format (Month Year or MM/YYYY)
  • Limit each position to 5-7 bullet points
  • Focus on achievements, not just responsibilities
  • Use present tense for current roles, past tense for previous

Education Section Guidelines

  • Include degree, institution, location, and graduation date
  • List relevant coursework for recent graduates
  • Include GPA if above 3.5 and you're a recent graduate
  • Add certifications and professional development
  • Include honors and awards if significant

Industry-Specific ATS Considerations

Technology Resumes

  • Include specific technologies and frameworks
  • List programming languages and proficiency levels
  • Mention development methodologies (Agile, Scrum)
  • Include project details and technical achievements
  • Highlight open source contributions or GitHub links

Healthcare Resumes

  • Include all certifications and licensure information
  • List clinical specialties and areas of expertise
  • Mention specific healthcare systems and software
  • Include patient care metrics and outcomes
  • Highlight continuing education and professional development

Marketing Resumes

  • Include specific marketing channels and tools
  • List campaign metrics and ROI figures
  • Mention marketing automation platforms
  • Include brand management and strategy experience
  • Highlight content marketing and SEO achievements

Final ATS Resume Checklist

Before submitting your resume, review this comprehensive checklist:

  • ✅ Single-column layout with simple formatting
  • ✅ Standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman)
  • ✅ Consistent section headings (Experience, Education, Skills)
  • ✅ Contact information prominently displayed
  • ✅ Professional summary with key qualifications
  • ✅ Work experience in reverse chronological order
  • ✅ Quantifiable achievements with numbers and percentages
  • ✅ Relevant keywords from job description naturally integrated
  • ✅ Dedicated skills section with categorized abilities
  • ✅ Education with degrees, institutions, and dates
  • ✅ Consistent date format throughout
  • ✅ No tables, text boxes, or graphics
  • ✅ File saved as PDF or DOCX
  • ✅ File named professionally (FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf)
  • ✅ Tested with ATS Checker tool
  • ✅ Proofread for typos and grammatical errors
  • ✅ Customized for specific job application
