ATS Resume Formatting: A Practical Review

Applicant-tracking systems are products configured by individual employers, not one universal test. A resume can be easier to read and parse when its structure is clear, but no format or keyword score can guarantee how a particular employer will process an application.

Use a structure that is easy to follow

Keep related information together and use familiar headings such as Summary, Experience, Education, and Skills. Put the most relevant information where a reader can find it quickly. Use consistent job titles, employer names, locations, and dates.

Decorative columns, text boxes, charts, and icons can make a document harder to read in some contexts. They may be appropriate in a portfolio or a role where visual presentation is part of the work, but do not use them at the expense of legibility or the employer's application instructions.

Tailor language without stuffing keywords

Read the job posting and note the tools, skills, and responsibilities that match your experience. Use the employer's terminology where it accurately describes your work, and explain important skills in context rather than listing unrelated terms.

For example, if a posting asks for SQL and you used SQL to prepare monthly reports, describe that task and the kind of data or audience involved. Do not claim a tool, certification, or level of expertise you do not have.

Review the exported file, not just the editor

  1. Export or print the resume using the file type requested by the employer.
  2. Open the exported file and check that text is not clipped, missing, or out of order.
  3. Copy its text into a plain-text editor and check that headings, dates, and bullet points remain understandable.
  4. Confirm that your contact details are accurate and easy to find.
  5. Follow any instructions about file type, file name, or required application fields.

What a keyword checker can tell you

A simple checker can highlight terms that appear in a job posting but not in your resume text. It cannot reproduce an employer's configuration, evaluate the full context of your experience, or know how an application will be ranked. Treat its result as a prompt to review the wording, not as an employer score.
